When in September nineteen ninety-six, after a boxing match in Las Vegas, members of two California gangs walked out of the arena, the most famous rapper in the country at the time — Tupac Shakur — punched a rival. He fell to the ground, and the crowd jumped on him. Shakur, along with the head of Death Row Records Marion Knight and members of the Bloods street gang, beat him until security appeared. The victim refused medical help and did not tell the police what had happened.
But he told his uncle, Duane Keith “Keffe-Dee” Davis. Davis, leader of the Crips gang, wanted to help his nephew get revenge. But, as stated in a report by the Bureau of Narcotics, based on a secret police interview, Davis said he had another reason to go after Shakur and Knight: according to him, Sean “[[Дидди|Diddy]]” Combs had put a one-million-dollar bounty on their heads. This information was reported by USA Today.
Yes, it seems that now the very person guilty of the death of the hip-hop legend has finally been found. But this case is not that simple. Let’s try to sort it out.

A few hours after the beating, shots were fired at Shakur from a car near the hotel. The rapper died a few days later. Knight received a grazing head wound but survived. (insert on background https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQHkjgOpFNlovM8xaB6ECV9fYtLffhp21Tsvw&s )

In twenty twenty-three Davis was charged with Shakur’s murder. The version that Combs ordered the killing was later highlighted by Davis’s lawyers, and around the same time federal authorities began investigating Combs in other cases. In new court documents tied to civil lawsuits against Combs, two more claims appeared: that he bragged about the murder-for-hire, and that he may have paid for the rental of the Cadillac from which the shooting was carried out.

The murders of Shakur and his rival — rapper Christopher Wallace, also known as Biggie, shot in March nineteen ninety-seven — have been debated in courts and media for almost thirty years. These cases spawned countless conspiracy theories about cover-ups, contract killers, and corrupt cops.

The fifty-five-year-old Combs has not been charged with Shakur’s murder and has repeatedly denied involvement. A Las Vegas police spokesman said Combs was never considered a suspect in this case. In a separate federal trial, Combs was recently prosecuted on charges of racketeering, human trafficking, and organizing prostitution. In July, the jury found him guilty only on two counts but acquitted him of the more serious charges of racketeering and human trafficking. Sentencing is scheduled for October. (insert on background https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQSpkBlFGHA2rH9-68GeNVbak0svfhXZbiUSg&s )

Combs’s lawyers are actively disputing the accusations linked to the new information about Tupac’s murder. They have filed defamation suits against accusers and media, claiming that “all pretense of objectivity has been thrown away, and the world audience is feasting on a mad banquet of lies and conspiracy theories.”

At the time of his death, Shakur was an artist at Death Row, run by Marion Knight and connected with the Bloods gang. Another hip-hop legend, Biggie, worked with the label Bad Boy Records, founded by Combs and associated with the Crips gang.

Both murders remained unsolved for decades. No one was ever charged for Biggie’s killing, and the alleged shooter has long been dead. Prosecutors in Clark County name Davis as the man who led the South Side Crips gang in Compton. Although he did not shoot himself, he is accused as the mastermind of the attack. Davis has pleaded not guilty and is awaiting trial. In nineteen ninety-four Shakur was on trial in New York for first-degree rape. While awaiting sentencing and needing money, he agreed to record a track for rapper Little Shawn. But at Quad Recording Studios in Manhattan, he was ambushed: three men shot him five times while trying to rob him. At the time, Combs and Biggie were upstairs, and later Shakur said they acted strangely. (insert on background https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcT-d2xhNmkBhISmbpYJUVft0J3iYrr0EEWk-g&s )

After the trial, when Shakur was convicted and sent to prison, his relationship with them deteriorated further. He tried to get money from Interscope Records but was denied. Then Marion Knight offered the company president to transfer Shakur’s contract to Death Row. After the deal, Marion posted a one-million-dollar bond to free Shakur pending appeal. At the nineteen ninety-five Source Awards ceremony, Knight announced Shakur’s move and publicly mocked Combs. According to former Los Angeles detective Greg Kading, this sounded like a direct threat from one “gang leader” to another. At the afterparty, a heated argument broke out between Knight and Combs. Witness Reggie Wright Jr., a former cop and Knight’s security guard, told the grand jury, quote:

In September nineteen ninety-five, Suge Knight’s friend, Jai Hassan-Jamal “Big Jake” Robles, was shot dead at the entrance to a party in Atlanta, where both Knight’s and Combs’s people were present. After that, according to Duane “Keffe-Dee” Davis, he and the South Side Crips gang agreed to provide security for Combs whenever he had to travel from the East Coast into enemy West Coast territory. (insert on background https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcQMdgX0qVJlZIXdQpgHUU0NfdM40iR3pKAn5Hnrj3qghfuUk5BDsQcz4VPQ1UnW5pTEEz0&usqp=CAU )

At the Summer Jam tour concert in California in nineteen ninety-five, where Biggie and other rappers performed, about forty Crips members guarded Combs, Davis claims. According to Davis, that was the night Combs first said he wanted to kill Knight and Shakur. As stated in the police report, he said something like: “I would give anything for the heads of those guys.”

Later, Davis writes in his book, Combs’s East Coast associate Eric “Von Zip” Martin called him and said: “Hey, Diddy said we need to meet. Come to Greenblatt’s Deli on Sunset.”

At the Los Angeles deli they sat on the terrace. When Davis went inside to order a sandwich, Combs followed. They stepped aside to talk privately. Davis claims Combs said then: “I have a couple of problems that need to be solved: the Big Boss and Pac.” According to police, it was about one million dollars for their murders. Similar accusations appeared in a two-thousand-three lawsuit against Combs, which was soon dismissed by a judge.

After Davis was charged with Shakur’s murder, he began retracting his words. He said the book was mostly written by a co-author and he hadn’t even read it, and that on the night of the murder he was at home in Los Angeles.

The boxing match between Mike Tyson and Bruce Seldon in September nineteen ninety-six lasted less than two minutes — about the same as the fight that broke out afterwards. (insert on background https://i.ytimg.com/vi/njAaHJ5yWPs/maxresdefault.jpg )

According to witnesses, the conflict had started weeks earlier at a California mall: one of the Crips had snatched a Death Row gold medallion off a Bloods gang member. Such medallions, gifted by Marion Knight to his closest people, were considered a badge of honor. (insert on background https://andscape.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/gettyimages-567430231.jpg )

When the gangs met in Las Vegas, the Bloods recognized Orlando “Baby Lane” Anderson, Davis’s nephew, as the thief. And they attacked him. After the fight, Anderson, Davis, and other Crips went to the parking lot. There, Martin (Combs’s associate) pulled a Glock from a stash in the car and said: “Time to make money.”

Later, in a white rented Cadillac, they saw girls shouting words of love to Tupac. The rapper leaned out of the window of a black BMW driven by Knight. The Crips turned their car and pulled up alongside the BMW. According to witnesses, Davis handed the gun to Anderson since he was the one beaten at the hotel, but it was awkward to shoot — the passenger was in the way. So Anderson passed the gun to Deandre “Big Dre” Smith, who opened fire. In other testimony, Davis said it was Anderson who fired. After the shooting, the car was disposed of and repainted, and the gun disappeared. Back in Los Angeles, Davis met with Martin. According to him, during a call he heard Combs’s voice, quote: “Was that us?” When confirmed, Combs, Davis claims, was thrilled.

About half a year later, on March ninth, nineteen ninety-seven, Biggie was shot at a red light in Los Angeles, where he and Combs had arrived from a party in different cars. Former police detective Kading, who later took part in the task force investigating Biggie’s murder, wrote in his book that he believed Knight had hired a hitman to avenge Shakur’s death. Knight himself denied this. Later that same year, Combs’s tribute to his late friend — “I’ll Be Missing You” — made him a superstar. Police received information that the Cadillac from which Shakur was shot had been rented in Compton by an acquaintance of the driver, Terrence “Bubble Up” Brown. However, at Nevada grand jury hearings in twenty twenty-three, former Las Vegas detective Clifford Mogg said he could not confirm this. The rental company the gangsters had used had long since shut down.

Meanwhile, in one of the many civil lawsuits against Combs, filed after his former girlfriend Cassandra “Cassie” Ventura Fine accused him of abuse in November twenty twenty-three, former president and co-founder of Bad Boy Kirk Burrows claimed that Combs had “arranged” Shakur’s murder and might have rented the car. In the current lawsuit, Burrows claims that he “heard Combs express paranoia and irritation over Shakur’s success, which reflected a deep need to diminish his rival’s influence.” It also says that Combs hinted to his people that Shakur’s presence in the industry threatened his legacy, and he had to “become increasingly aggressive to eliminate the competitor.” (insert on background https://cdn.i-scmp.com/sites/default/files/d8/images/canvas/2025/03/03/a00610fb-c929-4a3d-a3df-7e68741d7527_7a05e5c9.jpg )

According to Burrows, a week before Shakur’s murder in nineteen ninety-six, Combs hired several men to drive rented cars from New York to Las Vegas and back. When Burrows asked why, since it would have been cheaper and faster to fly them, Combs replied: “Shut up, mind your own business, and do what I told you.” In the end, Burrows booked the vehicles and paid the drivers one hundred fifty dollars per day, as well as covering hotel expenses.

Burrows’s lawsuit is not the only one mentioning a possible connection between Combs and Shakur’s murder.

In February twenty twenty-five, a man named Steve Otis filed a lawsuit, claiming that Combs bragged about his involvement in Shakur’s murder and used it as a threat. Otis said he flew from Florida to New York for intimate encounters with Combs and his partners in hotels. Such encounters were described in court as “freak offs.” In one such case at the InterContinental hotel, Otis claims Combs forced him to drink from a water bottle, rubbed him with baby oil, and that afterwards he felt drugged. Later, when Otis said he wanted to leave, Combs did not let him go and assaulted him.

Afterwards, the lawsuit claims, Combs threatened him, quote: “You better not say a word to anyone. You hear me? If I could take out Pac, then what do you think I can do to you?”

Combs’s lawyers said this is a lie and filed a motion to dismiss the case due to the statute of limitations.

The East Coast–West Coast rap feud ended long ago, and by general opinion, the victory remained with Combs and Bad Boy.

Death Row Records went bankrupt in two thousand six, and in twenty twenty-two its catalog was bought by Snoop Dogg. Marion Knight lost his shares in the company, at one point claimed he had only ten dollars in his account, and is now serving twenty-eight years for voluntary manslaughter committed by a hit-and-run in two thousand fifteen. (insert on background https://www.billboard.com/wp-content/uploads/media/suge-knight-court-april-30-2015-billboard-650.jpg?w=650 )

Meanwhile, Bad Boy Records, just part of Combs’s business empire, has sold more than half a billion records. Combs’s fortune, according to estimates, has fallen from one billion dollars to about four hundred million due to legal troubles. His acquittal on racketeering charges saved him from life imprisonment and asset seizure, but in October twenty twenty-five he faces up to twenty years in prison.

As for Davis “Keffe-Dee,” his trial is scheduled for February twenty twenty-six. He is the only living person who allegedly heard Combs offer one million dollars for the murder of Knight and Shakur. His testimony alone is not enough to charge Combs with ordering a hit, and there is no other evidence against him. Of the four men in that Cadillac, only Davis is still alive.
